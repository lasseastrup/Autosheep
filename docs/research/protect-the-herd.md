# Protecting a growing flock: escort, herd defence, and "the herd is the economy"

*Research for the survivors pivot ([`../PIVOT-SURVIVORS.md`](../PIVOT-SURVIVORS.md)), October 2026.*

**Method.** Compiled from web-search results and the search engine's summaries of the linked
pages; the pages themselves could not be opened from the research environment. A few checks
could not be finished: those items are marked **[unverified]** and rest on memory. Fan wikis,
AI-generated review digests and forum posts are flagged where they are used. (*Flock!*, the UFO
herding game, came out in **2009** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flock!)).)

**Main findings**
- **The niche looks open.** No survivors-like built around herding turned up. The closest are:
  - necromancer-horde survivors-likes (Be My Horde, Boneraiser Minions);
  - the *Escort Duty* missions in Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor;
  - the *Night Expeditions* in Pikmin 4;
  - *Herder*, a small 2021 game mixing herding and tower defence.
- **The protect-something designs players like share three traits.** Failure is gradual and can be undone. The protected thing only moves forward when the player is near it. Threats are signalled in advance.
- **The best "the herd is the economy" designs share two traits.** Damage costs economy before it costs lives, and the loss from any one wave is capped.

## 1. Survivors-likes and close relatives with something to protect, or with followers

### 1a. Protect-the-objective designs

| Game | What you protect | How you lose | How it grows / pays | What pushes you to defend it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor, *Escort Duty* (added at 1.0) | A huge mobile drill crossing the map | Drill destroyed | Doesn't grow | Narrow tunnels force you to fight right beside it |
| Pikmin 4, *Night Expeditions* | Lumiknoll mounds, until sunrise | Any one Lumiknoll destroyed | Glow pellets carried to the Lumiknoll become Glow Pikmin (3 pellets per Pikmin) | Your army spawns where you defend; decoy "Tricknolls"; the dog can be posted as a guard |
| Thronefall | Castle plus economy buildings | Castle centre destroyed | Morning gold from the buildings that survived the night | Destroyed buildings rebuild free but pay nothing that morning |
| Dome Keeper | The dome | Dome destroyed | Ore mined between waves buys upgrades | The wave timer drags you back from the mine |
| Kingdom Two Crowns | Crown, coins, villagers | Hit while your purse is empty | Coins buy villagers and walls | Every hit costs coins, tools or jobs (see §3) |
| Bad North | Houses on each island | All squads dead | Coins from defended houses | Houses are both income and healing (see §3) |
| Tribes of Midgard | The Seed of Yggdrasil | Seed destroyed | Loot gathered during the day | Going farther pays more but is deadlier |

- **Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor.**
  - PC Gamer called Escort Duty "a fantastic twist", because you can't escape the drill's path ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/deep-rock-galactic-survivor-review/)).
  - Analog Stick Gaming said it "forces you to rethink how you play" ([ASG](https://www.analogstickgaming.com/game-reviews/2025/9/17/deep-rock-galactic-survivor)).
  - Gamesear found it repetitive after a few dozen runs ([Gamesear](https://www.gamesear.com/reviews/deep-rock-galactic-survivor-review-version1-is-it-worth-playing)).
  - A Steam thread praised "no real timer and a straight path" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2321470/discussions/0/591782690120902124/)).
  - In the original Deep Rock Galactic, the drill's health is split into parts: left engine, then right engine, then core. Each part is gone for good once destroyed, and you repair by standing next to it ([DRG wiki](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Escort_Duty)). Not confirmed that Survivor works the same way.
  - Why players don't hate it: the drill moves predictably and never makes its own mistakes, so all the pressure comes from the horde.
- **Pikmin 4** ([wiki](https://pikmin.fandom.com/wiki/Night_Expedition), [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pikmin4/archives/418645), [Pikipedia](https://www.pikminwiki.com/Glow_Pikmin)). Losing a decoy doesn't end the night. This is the closest existing loop to "pickups grow the thing you protect, right where you protect it".
- **Thronefall** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thronefall), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/thronefall-islanders-tower-defense-strategy-steam-pc-1850701534), [Steam thread: a red X on a building means no income that morning](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2239150/discussions/0/3807282847927316422/)). A harder optional modifier restores only 25% of a building's health each morning ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Thronefall)).
- **Dome Keeper.** The developers describe the battle as both the reason to mine and a "show floor" for what you mined ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-dome-keeper-focuses-on-systems-that-feed-into-one-another)). Write-ups name the tension: come back too early and you waste digging time, too late and you take hits ([RetroStyle](https://retrostylegames.com/blog/why-dome-keeper-so-good/)).
- **Tribes of Midgard:** see the [Nintendo listing](https://www.nintendo.co.uk/Games/Nintendo-Switch-download-software/Tribes-of-Midgard-2261976.html).
- **Farm or herding survivors-likes barely exist.** The only one found is *Farmyard Survivors* (Viron Games, 2025). A cat, a bull and "an electrifying sheep" defend a farm across lanes, and the game has 9 Steam reviews ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3252160/Farmyard_Survivors/)).

### 1b. Followers and growing armies
- **Be My Horde** (Polished Games; Early Access since 2024; "Very Positive" on Steam)
  - Defeated enemies rise as your minions: peasants, knights, and sheep. The store page promises "hundreds of minions on screen" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2499520/Be_My_Horde/), [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/games/be-my-horde)).
  - Reviewers like that enemies feed your power. They also like the choice between swarming with weak peasants and saving energy for stronger units ([bgeek](https://bgeek.eu/reviews/be-my-horde-review/)).
  - The repeated complaint is control. An AI-generated review digest says players call the minions unaggressive, hard to control, and prone to clustering around the player. It also reports slowdown with big armies ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2499520/be_my_horde)).
  - Some reviewers say runs feel the same ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2499520/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated)).
- **Boneraiser Minions.** Each level-up adds a new minion type or upgrades one, and minions can be fused. Critics say the endgame narrows your build choices ([KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/boneraiser-minions-review-a-bone-afide-good-time/), [Jimquisition](https://www.thejimquisition.com/post/boneraiser-minions-boning-up-review)).
- **Undead Horde.** You can raise anything you kill, up to 100 undead. One skill path specialises in army size, and sacrificing part of the horde is a real tactic ([PlayStation Country](https://www.playstationcountry.com/undead-horde-ps4-review/)).
- **Overlord (2007)** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overlord_(2007_video_game)), [TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/Overlord))
  - The minion cap grows from 5 to 50.
  - Summoning spends colour-coded "life force" taken from kills.
  - Blue minions revive fallen minions if they reach the body in time.
  - One button recalls the whole group.
- **Little King's Story.** Citizens follow the king as a "Royal Guard" you throw at problems. Growing the city gives you replacements when they die ([review](https://superphillipcentral.com/2009/07/little-kings-story-wii-review.html), [forum thread](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/little-kings-story/52641)).
- **Snake-style games.**
  - *Nimble Quest*: heroes dropped by enemies join your conga line. Each has its own health and dies individually, but the run ends if the leader dies or crashes ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Nimble_Quest)).
  - *SNKRX*: each segment of the snake is a hero with its own attack and classes. Having 3 or 6 heroes of a class unlocks bonuses ([guide](https://gamepretty.com/snkrx-all-classes-units-and-items-guide/)). Here the group itself is your build.
- **Smaller precedents.**
  - HoloCure's *Uber Sheep* item drops food every 10 seconds (every 6 at max level). It's a sheep as a healer ([wiki](https://holocure.wiki.gg/wiki/Uber_Sheep)).
  - Spirit Hunters has more than 20 pets, each with its own "abilities, buffs and behaviours" ([PlayStation Store](https://store.playstation.com/en-id/concept/10006495)).
  - Brotato's turrets and other structures scale only with the Engineering stat, and you can have at most 100 ([wiki](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Structure)).
- **Checked, but nothing to borrow.**
  - Ravenswatch ([review](https://godisageek.com/reviews/ravenswatch-review/)) and Nordic Ashes ([GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/nordic_ashes_survivors_of_ragnarok)): no villager or escort content found.
  - Bounty of One: has co-op but no AI allies ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1968730)).

### 1c. How good escort missions avoid the hate
Players dislike escorts because they sit outside the game's main mechanics and take control of pacing away ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/can-we-fix-escort-mission-game-design-)). Tim Cain (co-creator of Fallout) adds three more problems ([Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2025/12/ive-never-seen-a-review-asking-for-more-escort-quests-fallouts-creator-comments-on-why-escort-missions-are-rarely-done-well)):
- hunting around for the NPC;
- not being able to match their speed;
- failing for straying too far.

Fixes that shipped in real games:
- **Make failure a process, not an instant.**
  - In ICO, shadow creatures drag Yorda toward a vortex. You can pull her out partway, and it's only game over once she's fully swallowed. The shadows attack Ico only to get to her. If you leave her alone too long, a special abductor called the "Cleaner" comes for her ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ico), [Team Ico wiki](https://teamico.fandom.com/wiki/Shadows_(ICO))).
  - In Resident Evil 4, enemies carry Ashley off, and a single shot makes them drop her ([Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-1578/)).
  - The RE4 remake replaced Ashley's health bar with a "downed" state you revive her from. She only dies if hit again while down. Capcom wanted her to feel "less like a second health bar to babysit" ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2023/02/02/why-capcom-changed-ashley-in-resident-evil-4)).
- **Never let the escort ruin your play.** In The Last of Us, enemies effectively can't see Ellie during stealth. This was a deliberate trade of realism for the player's attachment to her ([Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/how-ellies-pathfinding-made-you-love-her/), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/ellie-s-buddy-ai-in-i-the-last-of-us-i-explained-at-gdc-2014)).
- **Make the escort helpful.** BioShock Infinite's Elizabeth throws you ammo, health and salts when you run low ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/04/08/how-bioshock-infinite-s-elizabeth-makes-her-mark.aspx)).
- **Tie progress to the player's presence.** Overwatch's payload ([wiki](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Escort)):
  - moves only while an attacker is near it;
  - speeds up with more attackers, up to 3;
  - freezes when a defender contests it;
  - rolls back if abandoned, but never past a checkpoint.
- **Watch for players parking the escort somewhere safe.** The RE4 remake made Ashley always follow and removed her hiding spots. Leaving her in a safe corner "defeats the purpose of having her with you" ([VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/new-resi-4-remake-details-revealed-knife-combat-sidequests-ashley-sections-and-more)). This applies directly to pens and fences.

## 2. Sheep and herding games

- **Older herding games.**
  - **Flock!** (Proper Games/Capcom, 2009): you fly a UFO and herd animals into a mothership through physics puzzles. It has co-op and got mixed reviews (Metacritic 65–71) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flock!)).
  - **Sheep** (2000) and **Flockers** (2014) are Lemmings-style games: half-independent sheep wander toward deadly hazards and you steer them. Critics said Flockers copied Lemmings too closely ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_(video_game)), [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/flockers/flockers-pc-review)).
  - **Sheep, Dog 'n' Wolf** (US title: *Sheep Raider*) shows the predator's side. You play the wolf and steal one sheep per level, luring them with lettuce. A colour-changing icon shows what the guard dog can see and hear ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep,_Dog_%27n%27_Wolf), [retrospective](https://blog.gingerbeardman.com/2010/10/11/sheep-dog-n-wolf/)).
- **Herdling** (Okomotive, 2025) is the key modern reference.
  - The developers had two goals: guiding should feel intuitive, and the animals should feel alive enough to care about. They wrote that these goals "often pull against each other" ([developer write-up on Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/02/27/how-we-got-the-herd-in-herdling/)).
  - On numbers, the same write-up says too many animals make each one unimportant, and too few don't feel like a herd. So each animal gets its own fur, horns, size and sometimes behaviour, and flowers in the world let the herd stampede.
  - Reviewers praised the "gentle independence" and the feeling of "persuasion": you steer by walking behind the herd and waving a crook ([Good Game Lobby](https://goodgamelobby.substack.com/p/herdling-review-atmospheric-indie-adventure-calicorns-okomotive-panic-popagenda), [Final Weapon](https://finalweapon.net/2025/08/21/herdling-review/)).
  - Reviews say the herd tops out at about ten, and a bigger herd is harder to control and keep healthy.
  - Hazards and owl-like predators injure animals, and berries heal them. A second injury, or failing to rescue one at a cliff edge, can kill it ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/adventure/herdling-review/), [Gaming Nexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/16258/Herdling/), [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3555215353)).
  - Complaints: slow pacing, and frame-rate drops during stampedes ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/nintendo/herdling-review)).
- **Recent cosy herding games.**
  - **Flock** (Hollow Ponds, 2024): flying sheep trail behind you, the trail gets longer as more creatures join, and you shear wool to make clothes ([PlayStation](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/flock/)). It has no stakes on purpose, with no penalty and no score ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/flock-review-creature-comforts/1900-6418248/)).
  - **Sheepherds!**: co-op game where you play sheepdogs. "Sheep move and influence each other like real flocks", and you time your barks ([itch.io](https://ultimodisco.itch.io/sheepherds)).
- **Herder** (Porchman, 2021) is the closest precedent to the pivot ([ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/games/herder)). It has a tiny audience, but the structure matches:
  - tower defence plus herding: you guard sheep and goats with a dog;
  - you breed, milk and shear the animals, then sell the goods to passing caravans;
  - you build defences before entering each new pasture;
  - you can cash out at any time, or push deeper where predators, hunger and disease get worse.
- **Sheep tower defence.**
  - In *TowerMadness*, each alien targets one sheep and you lose when all are taken. The sequel gives stars based on how many sheep you saved ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TowerMadness), [TowerMadness 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TowerMadness_2)).
  - In *Operation Sheep Defense*, raiders carry sheep to exits, which gives you a short window to stop them ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2017/09/protect-your-sheep-in-operation-sheep-defense-a-fast-paced-tower-defense-game-thats-actually-good/page=1/)).
- **Upcoming games.**
  - *Medieval Shepherd*: you give your dog orders like "hold a perimeter" and fight wolves and bandits with a bow ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4608330/Medieval_Shepherd/)).
  - *Herding Adventures*: "you don't command sheep, you influence them… lose focus and predators strike" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4663000/)).
- **Shelter** (2013): you play a badger mother keeping her cubs alive against birds of prey, rivers, fire and starvation. A lost cub is gone for good. Eurogamer: "Rarely has a game articulated loss in such clear and urgent terms" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/244710)). **[unverified]** A bird's circling shadow snatches cubs caught in the open.
- **Not verified as existing:** "Sheep Patrol", "Baaa", "Sheepy", or a specific lambs-and-wolves arcade game.

**What makes sheep feel like sheep**
- **You influence them; you don't command them.** Every well-liked herding game steers through your position. A scientific model of real sheepdogs has two modes: **collect** stragglers when the flock is spread out, then **drive** the group once it's together. That model matches real sheep and dog data ([Strömbom et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4191104)).
- **Flocking rules plus fear.**
  - Start from the classic three flocking rules: keep a little distance, match neighbours' direction, and stay near the group ([lecture notes](https://www.cs.uaf.edu/2015/spring/cs482/lecture/01_26_boids.html)).
  - Make sheep run faster the closer a threat gets ([devlog](https://gibolinho.itch.io/shepherd/devlog/1434448/progress-at-the-beginning)).
  - Add **fear contagion**, panic spreading from sheep to sheep. A KTH thesis argues this makes flocks more realistic ([thesis PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1354921/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).
  - One devlog found that just circling the flock created "spacing, pressure, and rhythm" ([devlog](https://cravous.itch.io/sheep-sheep/devlog/1130271/finishing-sheep-sheep-a-10-day-sprint-of-herding-panic-ufos)).
- **The "selfish herd".** Animals at the edge of a group are at the most risk. In one study, the birds hawks chose to attack were about 35% further from their neighbours than others ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selfish_herd_theory)). Predators picking off stragglers is both natural and easy for players to read.
- **Food lures.** In Minecraft, sheep follow a player holding wheat, and wild wolves hunt sheep ([Tynker](https://www.tynker.com/minecraft/mobs/view/sheep/), [wiki](https://minecraft.miraheze.org/wiki/Wolf)).

## 3. When the protected thing is also the economy

- **Kingdom: money is your health, and damage strips jobs before lives.**
  - Each hit on the monarch drops a coin. With an empty purse, the crown is taken and you lose ([Greedling](https://kingdomthegame.fandom.com/wiki/Greedling), [Crown](https://kingdomthegame.fandom.com/wiki/Crown)).
  - A hit archer drops their bow and becomes a peasant. A hit peasant drops their coin and becomes a vagrant you can recruit again ([guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=740733723)).
  - Enemies carry off any dropped coins and tools ([guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1588497381)).
  - Even so, the creator "tuned the curve to make sure that every player would perish at some point" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-noio-and-licorice-s-i-kingdom-i-)).
- **Thronefall** caps a night's loss at one morning's income.
- **Bad North.**
  - Defended houses pay coins, which buy upgrades ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/bad-north-is-easy-to-pick-up-but-its-a-fiendish-strategy-game/)).
  - If a commander dies, their whole squad is gone for good.
  - Surviving squads refill on the next island or by resting in a house, and saving an island recruits a new commander ([wiki](http://badnorth.wikidot.com/commander)).
- **Brotato's "Harvesting" stat** pays out every wave and grows 5% each wave (8% for the Farmer character). In Endless mode it shrinks 20% per wave after wave 20. It's a snowball with a built-in brake ([wiki](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Harvesting)).
- **The Banner Saga** ([wiki](https://bannersaga.fandom.com/wiki/Caravan)):
  - your caravan uses up supplies every day;
  - starvation kills ordinary caravan members but never your heroes;
  - morale sets your fighters' starting strength in battle;
  - "renown" is both your experience points and the money for supplies, so you're always trading one against the other.
- **A steady supply of replacements.**
  - In FTL, stores sell new crew. That matters because losing crew weakens both staffing and repairs ([Gamicus](https://gamicus.gamepedia.com/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)).
  - In Darkest Dungeon, a stagecoach brings new recruits every week. Co-creator Tyler Sigman: "'loss' is not the same thing as 'failure'" ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/darkest-dungeon-and-permanent-death-in-video-games/)).
- **RimWorld scales threat to what you own.**
  - Raid size comes from your wealth plus an amount per colonist, multiplied by an "adaptation" factor ([wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)).
  - Players report that this factor drops after colonists die, giving "a breather", and then climbs back ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/3040481812832702908)).
- **Into the Breach.** Civilian buildings power the grid, and the grid is your health for the whole run. Every enemy attack is shown a turn in advance because the developers wanted "every death to feel like your own fault" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)).
- **Recovery windows and safety nets.**
  - In Sonic, a hit scatters up to 32 rings that you can briefly grab back, and holding even one ring prevents death ([Sonic Retro](https://info.sonicretro.org/SPG:Ring_Loss)).
  - Plants vs. Zombies gives each lane one single-use lawn mower as a last line of defence ([wiki](https://plantsvszombies.wiki.gg/wiki/Lawn_Mower)).
  - Pikmin 4 offers to rewind time when you lose a whole type of Pikmin or your entire squad. One reviewer called it "brilliant"; another often preferred to accept the loss ([Pikipedia](https://pikminwiki.com/Rewind), [Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/reviews/pikmin-4)).
- **Examples of a death spiral to avoid.**
  - In *They Are Billions*, a zombie that breaks a building infects its workers, and the infection spreads. One player says a zombie "turned 9 buildings before the game warned me" ([GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/daniel-weissenberger/they-are-billions-ps4-review/), [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/644930/discussions/0/3154186375125412035)).
  - In *Cult of the Lamb*, hunger, filth and deaths lower your followers' faith. Low faith leads to unrest and followers leaving, which lowers faith further ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/cult-of-the-lamb-followers/)).

**Patterns that stop the spiral:**
- spend economy before lives;
- cap each wave's loss;
- keep replacements flowing;
- scale threat with wealth, but ease off after losses;
- slow snowballs down late;
- warn players in advance so losses feel earned.

## 4. Growing from a few to many

- **Caps are a design tool.**
  - The Pikmin wiki explains Pikmin's 100-unit limit by performance and by design: numbers alone shouldn't win, and huge squads are hard to manage ([Pikipedia](https://www.pikminwiki.com/Special:MobileDiff/156991)).
  - Overlord's cap grows from 5 to 50, Undead Horde reaches 100, and Brotato allows 100 structures.
  - Herdling keeps its herd near 10 on purpose.
- **Keeping a big group readable.**
  - Make individuals look distinct (Herdling).
  - Show size as the length of a line (Nimble Quest, SNKRX, Flock).
  - Show a visible crowd that grows and shrinks. "Gate runner" mobile games such as Count Masters are built on this and have about 500 million downloads between them ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-method-in-the-madness-how-to-make-a-successful-gate-runner-game)). **[unverified]** Count Masters shows a number above the crowd.
- **Performance.**
  - Be My Horde reportedly slows down with big armies, and Herdling drops frames during stampedes.
  - Operation Sheep Defense puts more than 3,000 units on screen.
  - One known technique is "flow fields", described by Elijah Emerson. They move hundreds to thousands of units without calculating a separate path for each one ([Game AI Pro](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/9780429100277/chapters/10.1201/b16725-29)). The technique's usual link to Supreme Commander 2 is **[unverified]**.
- **How group size changes difficulty.**
  - Herdling: more animals are harder to keep healthy.
  - Agar.io: bigger is slower ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agar.io)), and fan guides say bigger players also shrink faster.
  - RimWorld: your wealth and number of colonists drive raid size.
  - Herder and Tribes of Midgard: going farther pays more and is more dangerous.
  - **[unverified]** Vampire Survivors' "Curse" stat trades more enemies for more experience. That's an opt-in difficulty knob a herd-size choice could copy.

## 5. Enemies that target the protected group

- **Thieves you can stop in time.**
  - In Defense Grid, aliens grab power cores and run for the exit. Killing a carrier drops its core, which floats slowly back home and can be grabbed again on the way ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Grid:_The_Awakening)).
  - TowerMadness and Operation Sheep Defense do the same thing with sheep.
  - Kingdom's flying "Floaters" ignore coins and carry townsfolk off to a portal ([wiki](https://kingdomthegame.fandom.com/wiki/Floaters?oldid=5758)).
- **Tug-of-war thief.** Pikmin 2's Breadbug drags food back to its nest. If it out-pulls your Pikmin, it drags them in and they die. It heals whenever it gets something home, and killing it releases everything it stored ([Pikipedia](https://www.pikminwiki.com/Breadbug)).
- **Grab-and-drop.** Pikmin's Swooping Snitchbug carries Pikmin off and drops them, which only replants them in the ground. Throwing Pikmin onto it brings it down ([wiki](https://pikmin.fandom.com/wiki/Swooping_Snitchbug)).
- **Scatterer.** Pikmin's Puffy Blowhog visibly breathes in, then blasts the squad apart, sometimes into hazards ([Pikipedia](https://pikminwiki.com/Puffy_Blowhog)).
- **Enemies that hit the back line.**
  - In Plants vs. Zombies, the Digger Zombie tunnels in and surfaces behind your defences, and the Catapult Zombie hits the rear-most plant ([wiki](https://plantsvszombies.wiki.gg/wiki/Zombies_(PvZ))).
  - In Age of Empires II, wolves attack villagers on sight. A cheap upgrade (Loom) lets a villager survive a single wolf ([Age of Empires wiki](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/File:AoE2_wolf.png)).
- **Warning done right.** Plants vs. Zombies' Bungee Zombie ([wiki](https://plantsvszombies.wiki.gg/wiki/Bungee_Zombie)):
  - marks its target plant with a bullseye arrow;
  - yells about a second later;
  - hovers for about 3 seconds, while you can still kill or freeze it.

  A defensive plant, the Umbrella Leaf, protects everything in a 3x3 area around it. One enemy covers the full pattern: a marked target, a window to react, and a structure that counters it.
- **Herds that fight back.** In Don't Starve, a decent-sized herd of beefalo usually beats a wave of hounds ([wiki](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Guides/Advanced_Hound_Protection)).
- **Night and fences.** Stardew Valley players report that animals stuck outside overnight are at risk of rare wild-animal attacks ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/0/1678064284145726932)). This comes from forum discussion, not confirmed game rules.

## Takeaways for a survivors-like about protecting a growing flock

1. **Losing a sheep should be a ladder, not a cliff.**
   - A hit makes the sheep shed wool, which is XP or currency you can pick back up for about 3 seconds (like Sonic's rings or Kingdom's coins).
   - A shorn sheep panics, and only a panicked sheep can be grabbed.
   - Only a sheep carried off the map is gone for good.
   - Every step before that can be undone.
2. **Make the thief your signature enemy.**
   - Wolves or birds drag a sheep slowly toward the nearest edge, with a visible line linking them and a marker at the exit.
   - Stagger or kill the thief and the sheep trots back to the flock (Defense Grid, Resident Evil 4, ICO).
   - Add a variant with a den, like Pikmin's Breadbug: destroying the den frees the stolen sheep.
3. **Warn players before every attack on the herd.**
   - Put a mark on the ground and play a howl, then give a 2–3 second window to react (the Bungee Zombie, the Blowhog).
   - Show an off-screen arrow and a bleat whenever a sheep is grabbed.
   - Never let losses pile up silently (They Are Billions).
4. **Make the flocking itself the battlefield.**
   - Predators go for isolated sheep, so keeping the flock together is the defence. The player's job is the sheepdog loop: collect stragglers, then drive the group.
   - Add enemies that scatter the flock, enemies that set off panic stampedes, and enemies that block the flock from moving (like Overwatch defenders).
5. **Make herd size the risk/reward dial.**
   - Wool and XP per second grow with the herd, compounding like Brotato's Harvesting but slowing late in a run.
   - Enemy spawns also grow with the herd (RimWorld).
   - After heavy losses, give a short lull, like RimWorld's adaptation factor.
6. **Grow the herd through safety.**
   - Lambs come from sheep kept grouped and unharmed, or from carrying pickups home as in Pikmin. That way, protecting and growing are the same action.
   - Cap how many sheep are drawn individually and represent the rest as a group.
   - Give a few special sheep their own identity (a lead sheep, a ram, a black sheep), as Herdling does.
7. **Cap the loss from any one wave.** Sheep scattered during a wave regroup in the next calm but produce nothing that wave. Only stolen sheep are lost permanently (Thronefall's model).
8. **Make the flock follow your presence.**
   - The flock drifts toward your lure and stops when you leave, so chasing XP away from it becomes a Dome Keeper-style gamble.
   - ICO even sends a dedicated kidnapper when the protected character is left alone.
   - Give a recall whistle (Overlord) and a sheepdog you can post as a guard (Pikmin 4's dog Oatchi; Medieval Shepherd's "hold a perimeter").
9. **Build in protection against frustration.**
   - Sheep protect themselves by bunching toward you.
   - The player never has to babysit sheep stuck on scenery.
   - No single lost sheep ends the run.
   - Add a once-per-run emergency move like Plants vs. Zombies' lawn mower, say a dog sweep that clears an area and pays out wool if unused.
   - Consider optional rewind-style insurance (Pikmin 4).
10. **Make sheep useful, not just a burden.**
    - Sheep upgrades can add food drops (HoloCure's Uber Sheep), rams that fight back (Don't Starve's beefalo), and per-sheep traits that combine into bonuses (SNKRX).
    - BioShock Infinite's Elizabeth is the target; Be My Horde's clingy, passive followers are the warning.
11. **Add fences, shelters and decoys, with a catch.**
    - Shelters protect an area (the Umbrella Leaf) or heal (Bad North's houses).
    - Scarecrow sheep act as decoys (Pikmin 4's Tricknolls).
    - Pens must not become a "park the flock and leave" strategy. That's why the RE4 remake removed Ashley's hiding. Penned sheep should stop producing, or attract tunnelling enemies.
12. **Use push-your-luck banking and partial success.**
    - Mid-run pens or markets let players bank sheep and wool (Herder's cash-out; in Tribes of Midgard, going farther pays more).
    - Score each run by sheep saved (TowerMadness 2's stars), so losing sheep costs points instead of ending the run.
